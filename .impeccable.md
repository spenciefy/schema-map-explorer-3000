## Design Context
### Users
A skier exploring a personal mountain project: enjoying the anticipation of weekends away, following snow, and choosing destinations. This is an exploratory prototype.
### Brand Personality
Tactile, curious, alpine. A feeling of discovery and a quiet longing for winter.
### Aesthetic Direction
An exploratory game atlas meets a sculptural mountain diorama. User references: Powder Mountain's 3D terrain and moving lifts; Werner Bronkhorst's miniature skiers on textured snow. Pale snow, evergreen ink, vermilion highlights, small colorful figures. Barlow Condensed display (ski posters / trail signs) with Manrope body. Reflex fonts rejected: Space Grotesk, Instrument Serif, Inter.
### Design Principles
- The map is the experience; interface sits at its edges.
- Real movement and small discoveries reward exploration.
- Weather visibly changes the world; demo weather is explicitly labeled.
- Real metric elevation and sourced trail/lift geometry preserve each mountain’s identity. Painted-map composition and illustrative trees/actors bring it to life; coverage and source limitations stay visible.
- Preserve keyboard navigation, reduced motion, and responsive controls.

### Art direction, refined
The user wants an art piece exploring trail maps and mountain atmosphere. Treat each mountain as an edition in a living collection: warm paper, large expressive mountain titles, quiet margins, forest green and faded vermilion, subtle print grain. The landscape owns the screen. Weather, passes, and lists live in a field guide opened on demand. Preserve accurate geography under the artistic treatment.
